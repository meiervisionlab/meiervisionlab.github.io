### graduate student opportunities

  *  **Current students** interested in working/collaborating with us should send an email or drop by my office (JDA 2147) -- if the door's open, I'm happy to chat.

  *  **Prospective students**: Graduate students (MS, OD/MS, and PhD) are admitted through [the UHCO's Graduate Program in Physiological Optics/Vision Science](https://www.opt.uh.edu/education/graduate-programs/). Applications are typically due end of January.

  * For those with access to funding/scholarship opportunities for exchange programs, we are delighted to host visiting students. Some examples of these opportunities might be listed under [resources](/resources/).

### undergraduate student opportunities

  * Our projects are well-suited for motivated students in any department who are interested in gaining research experience as they prepare for professional programs in optometry, medicine, or health; or those who are considering graduate school in psychology, vision science, and related fields. We can support student applications for [OURMA programs](https://www.uh.edu/honors/undergraduate-research/our-programs/), like the Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship or the Provost's Undergraduate Research Scholarship Program; reach out early if this is something you are interested in pursuing.

### doctor of optometry (OD) student opportunities

  * Currently-enrolled OD students can take part in research in the lab either by volunteering, [through the UHCO Summer Research Program](https://www.opt.uh.edu/research/summer-research-program/) (application deadlines are typically late April), or through the OD/MS program. If this is something you're interested in, you can email any time of year to discuss research opportunities.

### postdoctoral fellow opportunties

  * Are you looking for a postdoc position? We work on projects related to contrast sensitivity, binocularity/stereopsis, motion perception, and eye-movements; we’re specifically interested in how these things develop from childhood through adulthood, and how things go wrong when development is interrupted by disorders like amblyopia; and the tools we use are psychophysics, EEG, functional near-infrared spectroscopy, and eye tracking. Unless you're responding to a specific opportunity we've posted here, exactly which projects/tools you'll work on can depend a bit on your skills, interests, and career goals. Suitable candidates will either have overlapping interests in the topics we study (vision and/or how early experience impacts development), or overlapping interests in research methodology (including how to effectively use them in kids). We’re in an optometry department, but my own PhD is in Psychology and we welcome folks from any background.

  * We are always accepting inquiries for postdoctoral positions even if there are no specific positions posted here – keep an eye out for advertised (funded) positions, but if you'd like to put together an application for a competitive opportunity you are welcome to reach out. See [resources](/resources/) for opportunities you might be interested in.
